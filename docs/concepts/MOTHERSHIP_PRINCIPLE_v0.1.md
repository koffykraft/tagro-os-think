# Mothership Principle v0.1

**Status:** WORKING  
**Provenance:** SHARED

## Principle

A system should preserve a durable host of identity and continuity while allowing specialist capabilities to dock, change, detach, fail, or be replaced without destroying the whole.

## Farming interpretation

The farm or field is the mothership.

Possible docks include:

- land
- soil
- water
- weather
- crop and plant
- nutrition
- irrigation
- labour
- machinery
- power
- finance
- market
- harvest
- pest and disease
- buildings
- roads, paths and access
- household use
- records
- photographs
- sensors
- AI observations

No specialist dock should become the owner of the whole farm model.

## System consequence

A fact belongs to the mothership or the most appropriate authoritative dock. Other docks consume it by reference.

Irrigation, for example, may use soil, crop, water, power, weather and labour information, but it should not become the permanent owner of those facts.

## Design consequence

Interfaces may expose docks contextually, but the user should experience one coherent system rather than a collection of disconnected apps.

## Change consequence

A dock may evolve independently while the mothership preserves continuity and accumulated history.
